Music

Iranian classical and regional music traditions are built through listening, memory, technique, improvisation and long teacher-student transmission. The Radif provides a central repertoire, while regional forms preserve additional instruments, languages and performance practices.

← Iran Cultural GatewayResearch Notebook →
01 / RADIF

A repertoire designed for living performance

UNESCO describes the Radif as the traditional repertoire at the heart of Iranian classical music.

More than 250 gushe

The Radif organizes more than 250 melodic units called gushe into modal cycles.

Memory and improvisation

Musicians spend years learning the repertoire, then use its materials in improvisation and composition.

Oral transmission

The tradition has historically passed from master to disciple through sustained study and performance.

02 / INSTRUMENTS & REGIONS

Many sounds, many traditions

Iranian musical life extends beyond one classical canon.

Tar & setar

Long-necked lutes are central to Persian classical practice and appear in historical collections as well as modern performance.

Santur, kamancheh & ney

Hammered zither, bowed spike fiddle and reed flute each bring different timbres and techniques to classical ensembles.

Regional traditions

UNESCO also recognizes the music of the Bakhshis of Khorasan, dotar traditions and kamancheh craftsmanship and playing.
