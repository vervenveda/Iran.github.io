Art

Iranian visual culture includes manuscript painting, calligraphy, illumination, textiles, carpets, ceramics, tilework, metalwork, mirror-work and designed landscapes. These traditions changed across courts, cities, workshops and communities rather than forming one fixed style.

01 / MANUSCRIPT ART

Painting and the page

Persian manuscript arts joined text, image, paper, pigment, gold and calligraphy into carefully designed books.

Miniature painting

UNESCO includes the art of miniature among Iran's intangible cultural heritage. Miniatures developed in multiple regional schools and often illustrated poetry, history and courtly literature.

Illumination

Tazhib uses gold and intricate ornament to frame and structure manuscripts. UNESCO inscribed the multinational art of illumination in 2023.

Books as designed objects

Fine manuscripts can combine calligraphy, painted scenes, decorated headings, borders and bindings, making the book itself an integrated visual artwork.

02 / TEXTILE & SURFACE

Pattern carried across materials

Iranian design vocabularies move across carpets, textiles, architecture and garden planning.

Carpet weaving

UNESCO recognizes traditional carpet-weaving skills in Fars and Kashan. Materials, dyes, motifs and teaching practices differ by region.

Tilework

Architectural surfaces in cities such as Isfahan use geometry, vegetal ornament, calligraphy and color to organize large civic and sacred spaces.

Mirror-work

Ayeneh-kari, the art of mirror-work in Persian architecture, entered UNESCO's Representative List in 2025.
