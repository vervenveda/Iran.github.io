Legacy

Iran's legacy is plural. Archaeological sites, imperial records, religious traditions, poetry, cities, gardens, craft knowledge and oral practices come from different periods and communities. Continuity matters, but so do rupture, migration, exchange and political change.

01 / DEEP TIME

Many historical Irans

The word Iran links long histories, but ancient empires, later dynasties and the modern state should not be treated as identical institutions.

Ancient landscapes

Susa, Persepolis, Pasargadae and Bisotun preserve archaeological and inscriptional evidence across major ancient periods.

Late-antique Iran

The Sasanian period produced powerful institutions, monumental landscapes and long interaction with Roman and Byzantine worlds.

Islamic-era transformation

Persian language, literature, scholarship and art flourished through new dynasties, religious institutions and regional networks.

02 / LIVING MEMORY

Heritage still practiced

Legacy survives not only in monuments but in skills and social practices.

Poetry & storytelling

Ferdowsi, Saʿdi, Hafez, Rumi and other writers became part of Persianate literary memory far beyond modern Iran's borders.

Nowruz & seasonal traditions

Iran participates in multinational living traditions including Nowruz, Yalda, Mehregan and Sadeh.

Craft transmission

Carpet weaving, musical repertoires, calligraphy, miniature painting and instrument making continue through teaching and practice.
