# Source Evaluation Method

Ask: Who created the source, when, where, and why? Was it contemporary or retrospective? What genre is it—royal inscription, law, chronicle, memoir, diplomatic cable, oral history, archaeological report, or polemic? Is it complete, excerpted, censored, translated, or reconstructed? Do independent records corroborate it? Are key terms ambiguous? What did the author want the audience to believe? What do specialists disagree about, and what evidence could change the conclusion?

Do not use “biased” as a substitute for analysis. A royal inscription may be a valuable primary source for ideology and official claims even when its narrative requires corroboration.
