Editorial principles

History deserves evidence, not slogans.

This archive distinguishes original documents from later interpretation, state positions from independent corroboration, and cultural continuity from the changing borders and institutions of political states.

01 / SOURCES

Traceable evidence

Major claims should point to a document, inscription, dataset, archaeological report, or reputable scholarship.

02 / CONTEXT

History across eras

Ancient empires, Islamic-era societies, dynasties, and the modern state are connected, but not identical.

03 / ACCOUNTABILITY

Open to correction

Contested questions, uncertainty, source limitations, and corrections should remain visible.

Orientation timeline

Milestones, not a complete chronology

Use these periods for orientation; cite the records and scholarship for specific events.

Early societies

Archaeological worlds of the Iranian plateau

Regional communities and urban centers developed across different environments and periods.

c. 550–330 BCE

Achaemenid Empire

A major imperial system connected many peoples and regions.

224–651 CE

Sasanian Empire

A powerful late-antique state interacted with Rome and Byzantium.

1501 onward

Safavid transformation

Twelver Shi'ism became a central state institution.

1905–1911

Constitutional Revolution

Movements challenged arbitrary rule and demanded constitutional institutions.

1951–1953

Oil nationalization and coup

The oil dispute and Mosaddeq's overthrow became central modern events.

1979

Revolution and new constitutional order

The monarchy fell and the Islamic Republic was established.

2015 onward

Nuclear diplomacy

Track agreements through treaty texts, IAEA records, and UN decisions.

Research method

Separate evidence from interpretation

Core rule: official sources are authoritative for what an institution formally says, decides, or records. They are not automatically independent verification of every claim they contain.

Primary evidence

Inscriptions, constitutions, treaties, archives, objects, contemporary records, and testimony.

Scholarly interpretation

Research that explains evidence, debates methods, and weighs competing readings.

Transparent uncertainty

Document what remains contested, provisional, missing, or in need of corroboration.
