# 5. Safavid Iran, Qajar Rule, and the Constitutional Revolution

The Safavid dynasty helped consolidate a state whose rulers promoted Twelver Shi'ism as a central institution of political identity. This was a major transformation, but implementation and social consequences differed across regions and generations. Later dynasties inherited a changing political landscape.

Under the Qajars, Iran faced military pressure, diplomatic constraints, economic concessions, and growing foreign involvement, particularly from Russia and Britain. The Constitutional Revolution of 1905–1911 brought together varied constituencies demanding limits on arbitrary power, legal accountability, and representative institutions. Its factions disagreed about the content and limits of constitutional government.

Questions: How did Safavid institutions reshape religious authority? How did wars and treaties alter Iran's territorial position? What roles did merchants, clerics, intellectuals, regional groups, and women play? How did constitutional ideas circulate?
