# 7. The 1979 Revolution and the Islamic Republic

The revolution emerged from multiple sources of opposition, including religious networks, secular and leftist organizations, students, workers, bazaar communities, nationalists, and people dissatisfied with authoritarian rule and disruptive or unequal social change. These groups did not share one political program. Khomeini and his allies became dominant after the monarchy fell, and the new state combined elected institutions with powerful unelected religious bodies.

The 1980–1988 Iran–Iraq War deeply affected society, state institutions, security policy, and national memory. The Islamic Republic has changed through competition among political factions, elections, protest movements, institutional power struggles, and generational shifts.

Questions: Which groups participated and what did they want? How does the constitution distribute authority? How did the war shape public memory? How have women, workers, students, minorities, and regional communities experienced changes in law and public life?
