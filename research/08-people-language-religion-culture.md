# 8. Peoples, Languages, Religions, and Cultural Heritage

Iran is not culturally homogeneous. Persian is the principal official language, but Iranian society includes many languages and communities, including Azerbaijani, Kurdish, Luri, Gilaki, Mazanderani, Arabic, Balochi, Turkmen, and others. Language, ethnicity, citizenship, regional identity, and political allegiance do not always map neatly onto one another.

Religious history includes Zoroastrianism, several forms of Islam, Christianity, Judaism, and other traditions. Legal status, population size, visibility, and lived experience have varied over time and under different governments. Distinguish official recognition from community experience.

Cultural history includes poetry, calligraphy, miniature painting, carpets, ceramics, metalwork, music, gardens, architecture, irrigation engineering, urban design, and oral traditions. These developed through regional exchange, migration, patronage, commerce, and changing institutions.

UNESCO Iran heritage records: https://whc.unesco.org/en/statesparties/ir

Questions: Which communities are underrepresented in national narratives? How did Persian literature coexist with regional traditions? What can heritage reveal about exchange and everyday life? How have state policies affected language, religion, education, and preservation?

**Caution:** Do not assign a single political opinion or historical experience to an entire ethnic, linguistic, or religious group.
