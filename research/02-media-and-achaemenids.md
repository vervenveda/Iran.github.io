# 2. Media, Persia, and the Achaemenid Empire

Assyrian and Babylonian records, Greek authors, and Iranian royal inscriptions preserve different views of early Iranian political history. Cyrus II expanded from Anshan and defeated the Median ruler around 550 BCE; he captured Babylon in 539 BCE. Under Darius I, the empire developed a large administrative system and monumental royal program. It included many peoples, languages, religions, and local traditions and was not a culturally uniform modern nation-state.

**Primary-source focus**
- **Behistun inscription:** Darius's account of his accession and suppression of revolts, in Old Persian, Elamite, and Babylonian. It is primary evidence for royal ideology and official legitimacy claims, not a neutral modern history.
- **Cyrus Cylinder:** a Babylonian-language inscription describing Cyrus's victory and political-religious legitimacy in Babylonian terms.
- **Persepolis administrative tablets:** evidence about institutions, workers, provisions, and movement.

Research questions: How did imperial administration coexist with local institutions? How do Greek narratives compare with royal inscriptions and administrative records? How did kings use genealogy, divine favor, and order to legitimize power?
