# 4. Islamic-Era Iran and Regional Dynasties

The seventh-century Arab-Muslim conquests ended Sasanian rule, but social and cultural life did not change everywhere at once. Islamization unfolded over generations and varied by region. Persian language and literary culture later flourished within wider Islamic political and intellectual networks. Dynasties including the Tahirids, Saffarids, Samanids, Buyids, Seljuks, Ilkhanids, and Timurids shaped different regions and periods.

Mongol invasions caused immense destruction in some places and major disruptions, while longer-term consequences varied by location. Later courts also participated in artistic, scholarly, and administrative exchange.

Questions: How did Persian become a major literary and administrative language? How did Iranian scholars contribute to philosophy, medicine, mathematics, theology, and poetry? How did conversion, taxation, and local institutions change? How should accounts of destruction be balanced with evidence of adaptation and cultural production?
