# 6. Pahlavi Iran, Oil Nationalization, and the 1953 Coup

Reza Shah strengthened central state institutions and pursued extensive modernization while concentrating power and restricting opposition. Allied forces occupied Iran in 1941 during World War II, and Reza Shah abdicated in favor of his son. In 1951, Iran nationalized its oil industry under Prime Minister Mohammad Mosaddeq amid a major dispute with Britain over the Anglo-Iranian Oil Company.

In August 1953, a coup overthrew Mosaddeq's government. Declassified U.S. government records document U.S. intelligence involvement in planning and carrying out the operation; British involvement is also documented in historical research. The domestic political setting, Iranian actors, and long-term consequences should be studied alongside the documentary record of foreign intervention.

Under Mohammad Reza Shah, Iran experienced social and economic change, including land reform, expanded education, urbanization, and industrial growth. These changes coexisted with authoritarian rule, political imprisonment, censorship, inequality, and conflict over the monarchy's legitimacy.
