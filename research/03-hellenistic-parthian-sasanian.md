# 3. Hellenistic, Parthian, and Sasanian Iran

Alexander's conquest ended Achaemenid rule but did not erase local societies or traditions. Seleucid power coexisted with regional institutions. The Parthian Arsacid state became a major power between Rome and the eastern world. The Sasanians, beginning in the third century CE, developed a strong imperial system and made Zoroastrian institutions especially prominent, while religious and linguistic life remained diverse.

These empires were not simply successive versions of an unchanged state. They differed in administration, military structures, religious policies, and relations with neighbors.

Questions: What changed after Alexander? How did Parthian and Sasanian rule shape later political memory? What do coins, seals, inscriptions, and religious texts reveal beyond court narratives? How did wars with Rome and Byzantium affect border regions?
