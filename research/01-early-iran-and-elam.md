# 1. Early Iran, Archaeology, and Elam

This chapter covers prehistoric settlement, the Iranian plateau's varied ecological zones, early urban societies, and Elam, whose political centers included Susa and regions of southwestern Iran. Archaeological cultures, linguistic communities, kingdoms, and modern national identity are different kinds of evidence.

The Iranian plateau has a long archaeological record, with communities developing in different environments and at different times. Elam interacted with Mesopotamian states through warfare, diplomacy, trade, and cultural exchange. Susa's layered history cannot be reduced to a single ethnic or political identity. Written evidence is uneven, so some periods depend more on archaeology than texts.

Research questions: What do settlement archaeology and material culture show about regional networks? How did Elamite language and writing relate to neighboring traditions? Which continuity claims are supported by artifacts rather than later national histories? How did climate, agriculture, and trade shape settlement?
