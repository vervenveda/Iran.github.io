# Citation and Evidence Policy

For every substantive claim, record: exact claim; creator and title; date; stable URL or archive reference; page, paragraph, document number, inscription line, or dataset field; source type; independent corroboration; limitations; confidence; date checked; revision history.

## Evaluate sources contextually
- Contemporary documents can be incomplete, propagandistic, mistranslated, or written for a narrow purpose.
- Scholarly work should be assessed for expertise, citations, method, and engagement with counter-evidence.
- Government sources are authoritative for their own laws, decisions, and stated positions, but are not automatically neutral arbiters of all historical questions.
- Journalism can document contemporary events; major claims should be checked against primary records and independent reporting when possible.
- Unsourced social posts, decontextualized quotations, and unattributed translations should not establish high-confidence claims by themselves.

## Status labels
`documented`, `well-supported`, `contested`, `provisional`, `unverified`, `corrected`.

For ancient inscriptions, record the language, physical object, line numbers, edition, and translator. Identify whether text is a translation, paraphrase, or reconstruction. Avoid anachronism: do not project current borders, identities, or legal concepts unchanged into antiquity.

Corrections should be dated and explained rather than silently overwritten.
